1. What it is

SightLine is an installable web app (PWA) that photographs a document, recognises the text and reads it aloud — for people who cannot read the page, including people with visual impairment. It runs entirely on the phone: after the first install there is no network call, no upload, and no OCR library — recognition is our own 942,166-parameter CRNN+CTC network (37-symbol alphabet, digits/letters/space) shipped as a self-contained ONNX graph, with a build gate that fails the build if a third-party OCR engine reappears.

2. The measured results

What the bench is. bash run.sh bench-pipeline drives the real app — app/, unmodified — in Playwright WebKit, the engine iOS Safari uses, over 12 rendered documents (3 document types × 4 capture profiles: studio_clean, handheld_light, off_axis, jpeg_social), and writes one raw record per sample. ml/src/bench_hackathon.py then scores those records — the browser computes no metrics, so the two cannot drift apart. No sample is excluded: all 12 profiles are in the headline, 0 errors.

What this bench proves, and what it does not

Proves: the shipped app's entire path — preprocess, line detection, recognition, field extraction, classification — on these 12 rendered documents, in WebKit, reproducibly (bash run.sh bench-pipeline then bash run.sh bench-hackathon).

  • It is not a generalisation claim. The recogniser never trained on these renders, but the classifier's templates are in its corpus, so its end-to-end agreement here is not held-out evidence; the classifier's held-out figure is 95.8% accuracy / 95.7% macro-F1 over 5 seeds on n=24 documents, and it carries its own caveat — a near-perfect score on 24 documents means the task is easy, not that the model is strong (artifacts/step3_classifier_metrics.json, docs/14_HACKATHON_BENCHMARK.md).
  • Word accuracy is bag-of-words containment, not reading order. It counts how many ground-truth words appear anywhere in the read text after norm(). A scrambled read can score high on it. Treat it as recall of vocabulary, not as comprehension.
  • 12 documents is small (3 per profile). No per-profile difference here is statistically resolvable, and no confidence interval is claimed on n=3. The per-profile table is a description, not an inference.
  • The hard case is not in this table. The app was also measured with the whole-page Python harness on hand-photographed pages: on the 20-page set, detector recall 67.9%, line-exact 1.8%, CER 56.5%; the 193-page set-B reproduction of the shipped checkpoint reads line recall 71.6%, line-exact 2.4%, CER 58.4%. Both are real, on different page sets — and either way it is the honest number for real photographs, far worse than anything above (docs/14_HACKATHON_BENCHMARK.md; docs/03_RESULTS.md §3; HANDOFF.md §3).
  • These artifacts are frozen copies. Re-running bash run.sh bench-pipeline overwrites artifacts/pipeline_raw.json; the four stage files were copied aside at the moment each fix landed, so the progression stays readable. Re-running can only differ because the app changed — which is the point of publishing it.

3. What we got wrong and fixed

Four train/serve contract defects. Each one is a case where what the app fed the model was not what the model was trained on, and each one was found by measurement, not by reading the code. In this project the input contract has outranked the model every time (docs/18_CRNN_ACCURACY_STRATEGIES.md §0).

The four defects, in the order they were found, with the magnitude each one was measured to have.
DefectWhat was actually wrongMeasured magnitudeSource
(a) The exported graph was a fixed 256px Training built every crop at its natural width clamped to MAX_W = 320, and the corpus median is that clamp (of 3,270 held-out crops only 40 are ≤256px). The ONNX graph was baked at 256, so the app fed the model the first 256px of nearly every line — the tail was cut off before the model ever saw it. Crop-level exact match 44.7% at natural width vs 0.0% cut to 256; the read changed on 200 of 200 crops (PATIENT JANE DOE DOB 04021979 → PERIENT JANE DOE 2O08010; DATE 01102026 → DATE 01020). End-to-end, same 12 documents: word accuracy 3.6% → 22.4%, mean CER 70.8% → 57.5%, read tokens 468 → 680 (+45%). The graph is now exported at the training clamp, and the contract test asserts equality with it — the old assertion was width <= MAX_W, which any narrower graph satisfies, and it passed hardest on the worst value. docs/14_HACKATHON_BENCHMARK.md; app/js/recognizer.js; ml/src/export_onnx.py; ml/tests/test_graph_width_contract.py; artifacts/pipeline_raw_final.json
(b) Right-padding leaked through the backward pass The CRNN carries a two-layer bidirectional LSTM, whose backward pass starts at the right edge — inside the padding. CTC blanks the padded region for the loss, so training never noticed; at serve time the padded zeros moved the hidden states across the valid frames. Padded serve path 19.0% crop-level exact / CER 0.308 against 30.5% / 0.198 at natural width, agreeing with the natural decode on only 43% of crops; measured max |delta| 9.10 at content 64 and 15.28 at 200 (logit units), enough to change the decoded string. 74.7% of held-out crops are padded at all against the 320 clamp. Fixed by exporting a dynamic-width graph; padding is now a known loss, not a neutral default. app/js/recognizer.js; ml/src/export_dynamic.py; ml/tests/test_padding_invariant.py; docs/18_CRNN_ACCURACY_STRATEGIES.md §0.1
(c) The OCR candidate list replaced the preprocessed list With the restorer on, the app substituted the restorer's three candidates for the preprocessed ones — and none of those three is the raw image. So the ranking could only choose among three degraded variants of each page and never saw the unprocessed deskewed image at all. The ranking rule was innocent: raw wins on the rule's own objective once it is allowed to compete. On the app's own 12 documents, 145 paired crops, with the crop set, crop geometry and model held fixed and only the page transform varied: raw 45.5% exact line match vs 11.0% for the arm it was actually picking (tile-CLAHE 40.7%, CLAHE+Otsu 13.8%). The shipped ranking picked one of the three restored arms on 12 of 12 documents, costing 34.5 points of exact line match, 29.6 points of word accuracy and 9 of 24 fields — while not even winning on structure_score, the ranking's own objective (103 tokens against raw's 146). Fixed by prepending the raw candidate; the rule was left alone. app/js/app.js (comment and the one-line fix); app/js/pipeline.js (same fix for the non-restorer path)
(d) Deskew applied its own estimate with the sign inverted rotate() takes the angle in the opposite sense to the one estimateSkew() measures, so applying the estimate directly rotated the page away from straight and doubled the tilt. The estimator was never wrong: it returned the right magnitude, and re-estimating the app's own deskewed output returned 6.5–9.0°. Preceding that, the estimator scored each angle by a raw count of dark samples, which is not comparable across angles and left every tie to the sweep's first angle — a blank page scored −10, an axis-aligned page −8, a 4° page −7. It now uses a normalised projection-profile score, and the gate that would have caught the sign is a round trip (apply, re-estimate, require ~0), not “is the angle plausible”. off_axis word accuracy 1.1% → 45.1% (start → today); the old expression destroyed the two pages whose raw detection was already marginal (legal 3 → 0 bands, prescription 1 → 0). Bench-wide: word accuracy 35.0% → 45.9%, CER 0.465 → 0.303. app/js/pipeline.js (the negation and the measured off_axis table, both in the comment); app/tools/deskew_verify.js (the round-trip assertion); app/tools/offaxis_detect.js (records the 1.1% starting state and the shear-sign finding); HANDOFF.md §4 item 0; values computed from artifacts/pipeline_raw_rawarm.json and artifacts/pipeline_raw_deskew.json

Full detail, including the measurements that were tried and reverted, is in docs/18_CRNN_ACCURACY_STRATEGIES.md and docs/14_HACKATHON_BENCHMARK.md; the commit log is the primary record. Nothing in this section is a claim about the model — every one of the four was an interface defect between training and serving.

4. Why not Tesseract or Google Lens

The differentiation we claim is architectural and verifiable, not a benchmark win:

  • On-device by construction. There is no upload target, because there is nowhere to upload to: the service worker serves from cache only, inference runs in WASM on the phone, and after install the app works in airplane mode. A cloud OCR service (Google Lens and friends) cannot make that claim — the page image leaves the device.
  • No vendored OCR engine. Tesseract was removed; the recogniser is a network we trained ourselves. bash run.sh bundle-check fails the build if a third-party OCR engine comes back — a gate, not a promise (ml/src/bundle_check.py; docs/13_ECC_CODE_REVIEW.md).
  • Refusal over guessing. The app is built to say it could not read something rather than assert a value it is not sure of (see §6). That is the opposite of the default behaviour of a general OCR service, which returns its best guess with no notion of “do not act on this”.
  • Weights and algorithm we can read end to end. 942,166 parameters, 37 symbols, a self-contained ONNX graph, an in-repo export and a parity test against the browser decoder.

And the honest part, because a judge will ask it — now measured, not asserted. ml/src/tesseract_compare.py ran Tesseract 4.1.1 (leptonica-1.82.0, eng only, default LSTM, CLI) and our shipped graph over the same 12 documents, the same detected crops and the same ground truth: 188 scored lines (docs/24_ENGINE_HEAD_TO_HEAD.md; artifacts/engine_head_to_head.json). Word accuracy and CER are the repository's own definitions (ml/src/evaluate.py, ml/src/bench_hackathon.py). Line crops and whole pages are quoted separately because they are different integrations, not because one flatters us.

  • Tesseract wins the aggregate, and we say that first. 72.9% of lines exactly against our 11.7%, CER 0.134 against 0.197, 80.1% word accuracy against 61.2%. On a clean, flat scan it is the better reader, and no measurement of ours will change that (docs/24_ENGINE_HEAD_TO_HEAD.md §3 and §5 state the same thing).
  • The fairness correction — stated, not hidden. Our alphabet is 37 symbols (0-9, A-Z, space): it structurally cannot emit punctuation. 120 of the 188 scored rows contain punctuation, so they can never be a string-exact match however correct the read is — that alone is the whole distance between 11.7% and 41.0% line exact. On the identical-pixel control, punctuation-blind, ours takes 48 lines Tesseract misses and loses 16 (artifacts/engine_head_to_head.json, paired.identical_pixels_ours_vs_tess_arm_a_punct_blind). The strict column is not withdrawn — it is what an engine with no punctuation gets, and it is printed beside the blind one.
  • Where the split actually lands. On the one profile that models a real hand-held capture (handheld_light: defocus 0.5, ISO 400, light 0.08) ours reads 68.4% word accuracy and 83.3% of fields against Tesseract's 12.4–23.6% and 0–16.7% — a 5.5× word-accuracy gap, in the direction a phone app lives in. The mechanism is the binarisation stage between the pixels and Tesseract's net: leptonica thresholds a mildly defocused, noisy page, the strokes break up, and its LSTM then decodes plausible English over the broken mask. Our model is fed the grayscale crop directly, with no thresholding step.
  • The safety fact. On the clean page Tesseract read Rx Amoxicillin 500mg capsules as RX AMOXICILLIN 300MG CAPSULES — a plausible-word substitution of the dosage, on the exact field this product exists to protect. Its language model makes a wrong read still look like text, so nothing in the output says which word is the invention. Ours emits no character outside its 37-symbol alphabet (0.0% punctuation in its output), drops no line on any profile, and the app refuses a field it cannot verify (§6).

Plainly: Tesseract is stronger on clean, flat scans; we are stronger on real hand-held captures, we never invent a punctuation mark or a digit, and we degrade instead of hallucinating. We do not claim to beat Tesseract overall. The decision this measurement supports is which engine for which product: a vendored engine means a second WASM runtime plus its language data — eng.traineddata 4,113,088 B and osd.traineddata 10,562,727 B on the box it was measured on — and the build gate refuses to let an OCR library into the bundle (docs/24_ENGINE_HEAD_TO_HEAD.md §5; ml/src/bundle_check.py).

Superseding note: the earlier comparison recorded in this repository — Tesseract field accuracy 62.2% against our 7.4% at the best evaluate.py stage (docs/14_HACKATHON_BENCHMARK.md, “The honest comparison a judge should see”; artifacts/eval_results.json) — was measured before the recogniser was retrained and before the four train/serve defects in §3 were fixed, so it is misleading in both directions. The table above is its replacement, and it is the one to quote. Re-derive it with ./.venv/bin/python ml/src/tesseract_compare.py — 12 documents, 188 ground-truth lines, no number on this sheet taken on trust.

5. How to verify us in three steps

  1. Open the live app — https://adeviwon.github.io/SightLine/ — on a phone, and add it to the Home Screen. It installs.
  2. Turn on airplane mode. Nothing about the app changes: there is no network call left to make (docs/PRIVACY.md §offline, §airplane mode).
  3. Photograph a document and listen. You should hear the recognised text read aloud. We also suggest including one deliberately hard photo, where the app refuses — “could not read that, please check with a pharmacist”. That refusal is the design, not a bug (docs/13_ROUND1_SUBMISSION_KIT.md §demo run sheet).

6. The safety design

This app reads things people will act on — a dose, an account number, a date. A phone app must not be the last check on a dose: the consequence of an invented “500 mg” is a person taking the wrong amount, and nobody in the loop is in a position to catch it. So the design rule is refusal over guessing: when the app cannot read a document, it says so and asserts nothing. It still reports what it saw as text; it does not invent a value.

The measurement that forced the design. A confidence threshold is the obvious gate, and it does not work here. On a pure-speckle page (no text at all) the recogniser emitted glyph-like text — “EAX CE E 0RO 121G000 …” — at 0.678 confidence, inside the 0.687–0.974 band that real reads occupy, and the invented value it produced passed every shape validator: it was a legal 9-digit account number and a legal identifier. Neither available signal separates a hallucination from a read. What does separate them is document evidence: the speckle page scores 0 on every document keyword, while real reads score 1–17 even when they classify as the wrong type. So a read with no document evidence asserts no fields — the gate is on evidence, not on confidence. Measured cost of that gate on the 12-document bench: 1 of 12 documents affected, and it surfaced no fields anyway.

  • Rule and defect: app/js/pipeline.js (“A FIELD IS ONLY MEANINGFUL INSIDE A DOCUMENT TYPE”), the document-evidence gate, plus the follow-on fix that a model override only bypasses the gate when the model is confident (≥0.6) — a weak model opinion about a text with no document keyword is not evidence.
  • The same finding, recorded: docs/18_CRNN_ACCURACY_STRATEGIES.md §0.1 (“Confidence cannot gate correctness”, “What DOES separate a hallucination from a read is document EVIDENCE”); the band 0.687–0.974 is also the confidence range of real reads in artifacts/pipeline_raw_final.json.
  • Pinned by a gate, not a comment: node app/tools/warning_gate.js asserts the honesty invariants on all 12 samples, and node app/tools/e2e_paths.js covers the refusal path (HANDOFF.md §6).
  • No data leaves the device, so the safety story has no privacy side-condition: docs/PRIVACY.md; app/sw.js serves from cache only.

7. The policy anchor — and why it is the 2025 edition

The Open Challenge rule names one edition, verbatim: the problem must be “explicitly anchored in a priority from either the Hong Kong or Macau 2025 Policy Address that is not already covered by the five prescribed challenges” (official FAQ, read 2026-10-21). So the 2025 edition leads this sheet — every anchor below is quoted verbatim from the official 2025 text with that text's own paragraph number — and the 2026 edition is presented as the funded follow-through, never as the anchor. Full provenance, both PDFs and their SHA-256s: docs/19_POLICY_ANCHOR.md §0.1–§0.2; the 2025 PDF is policy-full_en.pdf, 118 pp, sha256 c4ecaf20…18f3ec.

Mapping strength, stated for the Q&A rather than discovered in it: ¶75 and ¶266 are strong (named AI-for-carers text; gerontechnology named as a remit), ¶227 is strong and vision-specific, and the stretched mappings this project deliberately does not use — 2026 ¶377 (rehabilitation places), ¶437 (internal government AI projects), ¶441–442 (critical-infrastructure cybersecurity) — are named as stretches in docs/19_POLICY_ANCHOR.md §6. Neither edition names the visually impaired anywhere; that gap is the open-challenge case, and it is stated rather than papered over (docs/19_POLICY_ANCHOR.md §7).
